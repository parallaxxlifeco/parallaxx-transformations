When I was a kid I figured out that if I built the best hut in the schoolyard, I never had to ask anyone for anything. No-one had to ask me for a spot in it or a turn in it either. I'd built it, so there was nothing to ask for and nothing anyone could say no to.

Thirty years on it's events, offers, resources, a studio. Ask me for something and I've probably already built it.

I want to say this first, because a lot of what gets written about this gets it backwards. It worked. That's why I've never fired it.

## Why is being needed so hard to give up?

You stopped asking for stuff a long time ago, and being the one who handles it grew up in the gap that left. And it pays. It pays in standing, in being the first call, in knowing exactly where you sit with people. You're never the one who owes anything.

A group member shared, "My need became the need to be needed."

That's a job description. And when people write about this they often write about it as a wound, aimed at somebody who is falling apart. You're the one everybody calls when they're falling apart. So the articles slide off, because they're describing somebody else.

What you're doing is a role, you're good at it, and giving it up would mean giving up being the one everybody comes to. Being told it's just people-pleasing is going to make you close the tab.

## So why doesn't giving less work?

Because giving less changes how much you do, and the reflex underneath stays the same.

You can do fewer favours this month and still, when somebody mentions a problem, you're three steps into solving it before you've decided to. That reflex happens by itself, and it doesn't change because you had a think about your capacity on Sunday.

Another group member pointed this out: "How do I fix the need of fixing?" She heard the loop in it herself while she was saying it.

And the loop is real. Every tool she has is for taking things on, processing things and managing things, so the only tool available for the problem of taking on too much is taking on more.

## What happens when there's nothing to fix

There is a clue, and it's uncomfortable.

Somebody close to you gets hit with a problem you can do nothing about, the kind with no solution in it. And underneath the worry there's a thought you'd rather not have had. You catch yourself wanting the old problem back, the one you could do something about.

That's what happens when being useful is the way you know how to be close to someone. Take the usefulness away and you're standing there with empty hands in front of somebody you love, and it's unbearable.

I watched a participant share a really hard thing that had happened to her that week, ask the group for nothing at all, and then give everybody three exercises. They were excellent exercises, and they were also her way out of the conversation.

## What happens when no-one gets to give you anything?

The other side of this takes years to show up.

You get there first, most times, and faster than anybody else would have. So the people around you stop offering, because their offer kept coming after the thing was already done. There's nothing left for them to give.

And then the evidence stacks up on your side. Nothing gets offered, so it must be true that you're the only one who does this around here.

What a lot of these women want, when you get underneath it, is the thing that needs another person to show up and do it. No amount of your excellence produces that. You can't get there first on it.

## So what do we do instead?

Two things, and they're both small.

**First, leave a task undone.** There's a task near you that somebody else could handle and that you'd normally have finished by now. Leave it, and say nothing about leaving it. Then watch what they do, and let it be done the way they do it rather than the way you'd have done it.

Receiving only in the form you'd have chosen is just ordering.

**Second, ask for what you need where you're the least capable person there.** Saying yes is easy where you're already good. Somebody gives you a challenge in your field and of course you'll take it, you've got this, you know it. The challenge that really matters is where you're the least confident and the least capable in the group, and where you're doing it for you rather than for somebody else.

That's where the asking is too, for the kind of request you couldn't have covered yourself.

I'm still working this one. Mine goes like this:

> I don't ask for what it is that I want because I want to caretake their emotions that I'm perceiving they're going to have. But that's not my responsibility. And so then I don't ask for it.
> — Daniel Lawson

I get in front of somebody's feelings before they've had them, decide it'd be a burden on them, and then handle it myself. It's very considerate, and it's also how I never find out.

## Common questions

### Isn't wanting to be needed a good thing?

Being useful is a good thing and it's a lot of why people trust you. Look at whether it's the only way you know to be close to people. If being needed is the way you know how to be close to someone, then every relationship you're in has to keep producing problems for you to solve.

### How do I know if I'm doing it?

Watch the speed. If somebody mentions a problem and you're solving it before they've finished the sentence, that's the clue. The other clue is getting asked what you need and coming up with nothing, while you can say what everyone else in your life needs without pausing.

### Where does it come from?

Often from a house where feelings didn't get much airtime, so you became the one who handled it instead. That's worth knowing and it won't change anything by itself. Knowing where it started and interrupting it are two different skills.

### What if they really do depend on me?

They probably do, and that's real. The question is whether there's a task in there that could be somebody else's, and what you notice in yourself while you're trying to hand it over.

### How do I catch it in the moment rather than afterwards?

A participant shared the question she asks herself: whether this is her responsibility right now, or whether she's going overboard with the giving again. Asking it while you're already in action is the practice. Many people get there three hours late for a while, and that still counts.

## If this is you

If you're the one everybody calls, you already know how to show up for people when it counts. What comes next is letting that happen the other way, somewhere you don't have to be the capable one.

A woman shared with me that what she wanted was to be in a place where she didn't have to be the capable one. And she said it in a group where somebody else was running the thing, which is a big reason it got said at all.

In the group, somebody else is running it, so for that time you're not the one who has to. And then you get to find out what's underneath the job, which is usually a want you put down so long ago you forgot it was ever yours.
