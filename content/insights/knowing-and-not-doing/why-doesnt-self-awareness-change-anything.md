For years I told people I needed a woman who would command my attention. Not demand it. Command it.

I was pretty pleased with that line. It felt like self-knowledge. I'd worked out something true about myself, I could say it clearly, and it explained a lot about why relationships had gone the way they'd gone.

Then a guy said to me, well, aren't you just outsourcing your responsibility?

Took me over a year to get what he meant. What I had wasn't self-knowledge. It was a job description for somebody who hadn't turned up yet. I'd written a very articulate account of why the thing I wasn't doing was somebody else's job.

And that's awareness for you. It can be completely accurate and still be working for the other side.

## Describing it is the easy part

Most people I sit with can narrate their own pattern better than I can. They know when it started. They know who taught it to them. They've read the book, done the course, maybe sat with a therapist for a few years and got genuinely good at the language.

And they're still in it. All of them. Including me, on the things I haven't sorted yet.

A lot of my industry is built on that gap and doesn't mention it. Read the textbooks, learn the strategies, analyse it well enough and you get to call it handled. It's a bypass. It's a very comfortable one, because analysing is the part you're already good at, and it produces the feeling of progress without any of the exposure that change comes with.

I've said the same thing to the same man twice, months apart, and watched him have the same realisation both times. Both times it was real. Neither time did anything move. At some point you have to stop being impressed by the insight.

## So why am I good at thinking and bad at doing?

Because thinking never costs you anything. And it's worth being blunt about why.

When you analyse something you stay in the one place where you have never lost. You're capable, you're articulate, you can hold four angles at once. Nobody has ever told you that you weren't clever enough, and thinking is where being clever pays out.

Doing is different. Doing puts you somewhere you might be wrong in front of people. It has a result, and the result might not be the one you wanted, and then you'd have to sit in that.

So of course the analysis gets more sophisticated every year. You're practising the safe one.

And learning is the most respectable way to not start. Nobody will ever challenge you on it. Read another book, do another course, wait until you understand it properly, and every single one of those looks like diligence from the outside. From the inside it's the same delay, and this time it looks sensible.

## The pattern is not a mistake

This is the part that reframes it for most people.

You're treating the pattern like an error, something you'd stop doing if only you understood it properly. So you understand it harder. More reading, more reflection, more naming.

But it was never an error. It's a solution. It got built for a reason, it solved something real at the time, and it's still solving it now.

> it's the protective parts of ourselves doing a wonderful job of not helping us cause more pain within ourselves so we can feel good in the moment, but it keeps us stuck
> — Daniel Lawson

Which means understanding it doesn't retire it. If anything it makes it more sophisticated, because now it can use your own language back at you. The most self-aware people I know have the most articulate avoidance.

Mine is storytelling. One of the easiest ways we protect ourselves from the answers we say we're looking for is over-complicating it by telling stories, and I know that because it's one of my biggest protective mechanisms. I can talk my way around problems. I can confuse the shit out of coaches, because I can lead them astray just so they can't see who I am and what my real challenges are.

What a lot of people do when they finally see the mechanism is go, right, I've got it now, I'll just try harder. And trying harder against a protective part is like arguing with your own immune system. It's doing what it was built to do and it will win, because it's older than your plan.

## Overthinking is not a thinking problem

So many of the things that feel stuck are stuck because they're living in your head, going round.

And the instinct, when something is stuck in your head, is to think about it more carefully. Which is like trying to get out of a hole with a better shovel.

What breaks it is moving faster than is comfortable. Not recklessly. Just quicker than the point where your mind wants another lap. Because the extra lap isn't producing new information, it's producing the feeling of having done something, and that feeling is what you're going back for.

Watch yourself next time. The fourth time round a problem, are you learning anything? Or does it just feel better than the alternative, which is going and finding out?

## So what does move it

The moment before.

Not the pattern. The two or three seconds in front of it, where you feel the pull and there's a choice sitting there that you don't normally clock. Your body usually gets there first, a beat ahead of your thinking. Tight chest, the urge to change subject, the sudden very good reason to check your phone.

That's the only place any of this is available. Everything after it is commentary.

So the practice is small and repetitive and nothing like the insight work you're used to:

1. Find the tell. Everyone has one. Mine's above.
2. Catch it once. Not stop it. Just notice it while it's happening rather than three hours later.
3. Do the other thing once. Say the sentence, ask the question, stay in the conversation eleven seconds longer than is comfortable.
4. Notice what happened. Not what you predicted. What did.

That's four reps, not four realisations. Reps are boring, which is why almost nobody writes about them and why the market keeps selling you the realisation instead.

## Acknowledge yourself for the attribute, not the outcome

What I almost never see, though is this. Someone capable goes and does a genuinely hard thing, and then says, I'm proud of myself for that.

It's rare. Successful, independent people hit the target and move straight to the next one, and the doing never gets banked, so it never becomes evidence of anything.

So when you do a rep, name what it required of you rather than what it produced. Not I sent the message. I said what I wanted without softening it first, and I didn't take it back when it went quiet for an hour.

That's the sentence that builds something. The outcome is out of your hands anyway. What it took from you is yours.

## Common questions

### So is self-awareness useless?

No, it's necessary and it isn't sufficient. Without it you can't see the moment coming at all. With it and nothing else you get a very good seat for watching yourself do the same thing again.

### Isn't preparation important?

Yes, and there's a point where it stops being preparation. The test is whether the next hour of thinking will change what you do. If you already know what you'd do and you're refining how you'd feel about it, that's not preparation.

### Why does it feel like I'm going backwards?

Because you can see it now, and you couldn't before. The behaviour hasn't got worse, the visibility has got better, which is uncomfortable and is also the first honest measurement you've had.

### I've tried forcing myself and it doesn't stick. Now what?

Forcing tends to fail because the target was too big. Shrink it until it's almost embarrassing, then do that. Momentum built from small completed things holds; momentum built from one heroic effort doesn't survive the week you're tired.

## If this is you

The thing underneath most of it is that you stopped asking for what you wanted a long time ago and got very good at not needing anything, and awareness is a comfortable place to sit while continuing not to ask.

Which is why I run groups rather than only working one to one. You can't catch your own moment on your own. Somebody else in the conversation sees it a few seconds before you do, and says so, and then it's in front of you while it's still happening rather than three hours later.
